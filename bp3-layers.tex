\section{The layer architecture}\label{sec:layers}

The proof paper is organised as eight layers, in the sense Brady uses for
building up a field: each layer consumes the \emph{interface} of the layers
below it --- their statements, with exactly the hypotheses those statements
carry --- and exports a small interface upward, and no proof may cite a
statement of a higher layer. The assignment of every theorem-like statement to
a layer lives in \texttt{layers.tsv}; \texttt{layercheck.py} recomputes the
proof-dependency graph and fails the build on any upward citation, so the
discipline is machine-checked on every run of \texttt{check.sh}, alongside
acyclicity.

The point of declaring the boundaries is that every serious error this project
has produced or found is an interface violation in disguise: a proof consuming
a fact about the layer below that its interface does not state
(\inproof{haz:rectangularise}, before it was closed); a caller ignoring a
dotted return (\inproof{haz:pp-dotted}); a definition changed without
re-checking its consumers (the single-step cruciality episode, corrected the
same day); a conjunction read as a disjunction (\inproof{haz:S-type}). Declared
boundaries make this class of error checkable rather than discoverable.

``Exported'' counts statements cited from a strictly higher layer;
``open debts'' counts statements not \textsc{proved} or \textsc{imported} in
the status table of the proof paper. The starred counts are \emph{lower
bounds}: an interface is only as visible as the proofs that consume it, and
the biggest consumers of L3 and L5 --- \inproof{lem:bridge-from-instance} and
\inproof{thm:t1} --- have no proofs yet, so their edges do not exist. Writing
L4 out will widen L3's interface substantially; that widening is worth
watching, since every new export is a new obligation on L3's statements.

\subsection{What each layer provides, owes, and imports}

\begin{description}\tightlist

\item[L0 --- foundations.] Universal algebra and pure relation combinatorics:
clones and generation, linking equivalences, rectangularity of relations,
$\Vn$ and the arithmetic of $\mathbf Z_{p}$ therein
(\inproof{prop:p-divides}, \inproof{lem:subuniverse-coset}), the two purely
combinatorial workhorses \inproof{lem:multiset-shift} and
\inproof{lem:full-fibre}, and the classical imports: Bergman for generation by
terms, Hobby--McKenzie for abelian $=$ affine, Mar\'oti--McKenzie for the
special WNU, Geiger and Bodnarchuk et al.\ for Inv--Pol. No open debts.

\item[L1 --- absorption.] Binary absorbing, central, and $\TS$; everything
about them that never mentions an irreducible congruence. Its fifteen-wide
interface is the largest in the document, and its heaviest members are the two
substantial imports: the Absorption Theorem (\inproof{thm:brady-absorption})
and the central-relation source (\inproof{thm:central-relation-source}).
Sources: Brady's notes \S3.10--3.11 prove both; Barto--Kazda's \emph{Deciding
Absorption} proves \inproof{lem:pp-propagation} and
\inproof{lem:barto-kazda}; the remaining imports are Zhuk's strong-subalgebras
paper (Lem.~3.2, 3.4, 6.8, 6.11, 6.24, 6.25, Cor.~6.1.2, 6.9.2, 6.11.1). No
open debts.

\item[L2 --- congruences and bridges.] Irreducible, linear and PC congruences,
covers, bridges and their calculus (composition, symmetrisation,
reflexivity, rectangularisation), and the bridge classification:
\inproof{lem:linear-equiv}, \inproof{lem:no-cross-bridge},
\inproof{lem:pc-bridges-trivial}, \inproof{lem:bridge-to-pc}. Imports are
three lemmas of the JACM paper (Lem.~6.3, 7.2, Cor.~8.17.1). Debts:
\inproof{lem:block-good-bridge} is outlined, and \inproof{lem:pc-on-top} is
stated (consumed by nothing).

\item[L3 --- strong subuniverses.] The six types, $\lll$, propagation,
the intersection property, stable intersection. Two facts distinguish this
layer. \emph{It imports nothing}: every one of its 27 statements is the
simplified paper's own contribution, with no external source to fall back on.
And \emph{it carries both open hypothesis-debts}:
Hypothesis \pkey{hyp:maximal-mult} and Claim \pkey{cl:multiply-step}, each
written into a statement and exported upward as a precondition rather than
hidden in a proof. Debts: four outlined (\pkey{lem:factor-by-delta},
\pkey{lem:center-pushed-in}, \pkey{lem:leftlinked-stay-full},
\pkey{lem:multiply-all-linear}), four stated (the three propagation
corollaries and \pkey{lem:preserve-linked}), plus the two hypotheses.

\item[L4 --- instances.] Consistency, coverings, cruciality, weakening.
Imports: JACM Lem.~6.1 (\inproof{lem:expanded-consistency}) and
strong-subalgebras Lem.~5.6 (\inproof{lem:tree-coverings}). Debts: eight of
its thirteen statements are stated only, including
\inproof{lem:bridge-from-instance} --- the sole consumer-to-be of stable
intersection, and the largest single piece of unwritten mathematics in the
document.

\item[L5 --- main statements.] The main induction, reductions-safe, PC-safe,
codimension one, and the $\Omega$ construction. Debts: three outlined, one
stated. The uniform-witness check of \inproof{haz:witness-moved} lives at
this layer's boundary with L4: L5 now hypothesises a uniform type-$\TBA$
reduction, and the producers that must be checked to supply one are L4's.

\item[L6 --- algorithm, hard half, assembly.] $\textsc{Solve}_{\mathrm{alg}}$
and its termination, target (T1), the NAE interpretation and (H0), the
core/rigid-core reduction. Import: Brady \S1.4. Debt: \inproof{thm:t1}.

\item[L7 --- complexity.] The two wrappers, NP membership, and the dichotomy
itself. Everything here needs a cost model, and nothing below it does; the
layer exists to keep that boundary sharp. Debts: all but
\inproof{thm:dichotomy}, which is proved from its rigid-core form.

\end{description}

\subsection{The first dividend}

The check paid for itself on its first run. It reported a single upward
edge: \pkey{cor:no-central-\brk critical}, assigned to L1 by content, cited the
$\lll$-pullback lemma \pkey{lem:reverse-hom} from L3 --- and through it the
whole bridge classification of L2 rested on the strong-subuniverse layer,
which would have forced the two layers to merge. Inspection showed the $\lll$
hypotheses of \pkey{lem:no-central-critical} and its corollary are consumed by
nothing in either proof. Narrowing the statements to the hypotheses actually
used, and rerouting the corollary's pullback through
\pkey{lem:pp-propagation}, removed the edge; the bridge classification
genuinely sits below strong subuniverses. That is the methodology working as
intended: the violation was found mechanically, and the fix was an API
narrowing, not new mathematics.
