# 1. Definition list (verbatim, with formalization notes)

Throughout, the **standing global hypothesis** stated at main.tex:1123–1125 is:

> "In this paper we assume that every algebra is a finite idempotent algebra having a WNU term operation."

and (main.tex:1641–1642, opening of 2.3):

> "Recall that all the algebras in the following statements are assumed finite idempotent algebras having a WNU term operation (Taylor)."

*Formalization note.* This is a blanket hypothesis, never repeated in individual statements. Every Lean statement in this section must carry `[Finite A] [Idempotent A] (hW : HasWNUTerm A)` (or a structure/typeclass bundling them). A handful of statements additionally require `A ∈ 𝒱ₙ` (special WNU), namely Lemmas 5, 11, 27; and one requires `Zₚ` (Lemma 29). Nothing in Section 2 is claimed for algebras without a WNU.

### D1. `[k]`
"For a positive integer `k` by `[k]` we denote the set `{1,2,…,k}`." (L1078)

### D2. Special WNU
"An idempotent WNU `w` is called **special** if
`w(x,…,x,y) = w(x,…,x,w(x,…,x,y))`." (L1079–1080)

Background (L408–410): "An operation `f` on a set `A` is called a **weak near-unanimity (WNU) operation** if it satisfies `f(y,x,…,x) = f(x,y,x,…,x) = … = f(x,x,…,x,y)` for all `x,y ∈ A`." Idempotent (L778): `f(x,…,x)=x`.

*Formalization notes.*
- The special identity has the `y` in the **last** position only; combined with the WNU identities this makes it hold in every position, but that must be derived, not assumed.
- WNU is stated as a chain of `n` equalities among the `n` "one-`y`" tuples; no relation to `f(x,…,x)` is asserted, idempotence is separate.
- "It is not hard to show that for any idempotent WNU `w` on a finite set there exists a special WNU `w' ∈ Clo(w)`" — this is Lemma 26 = Maróti–McKenzie [miklos, Lemma 4.7], an **external import** with an explicit arity `nⁿ!`.
- `Clo(w)` = clone generated by `{w}` (term operations of `(A;w)`); `Clo(A)` = clone of term operations of `A`. Never formally defined in the paper — it is assumed universal-algebra background from Bergman.

### D3. Algebra conventions (L1088–1101)
Bold letters for algebras, plain for domains; `B ⩽ A` means "**B** is a subalgebra of **A**"; a congruence is **nontrivial** if it is neither the equality relation nor `A²`; `0_A` is the equality relation on `A`, "which is the 0-congruence on **A**"; "to avoid overusing of bold symbols sometimes we write capital symbol meaning the algebra".

*Formalization notes.*
- **`B ⩽ A` vs `B ⊆ A`.** The paper deliberately conflates subalgebra and subuniverse ("capital symbol meaning the algebra"). In the definitions of subuniverse types, `C`, `B`, `D` are *sets* that are simultaneously treated as algebras. A Lean design must pick one (I recommend: `Subuniverse A` = a subset closed under all term ops, coerced to an algebra when needed) and then be disciplined about `B/σ`, `C ∩ D`, `f(C)`, `pr₁(R∩…)` all being subuniverses.
- **Empty set.** For an idempotent algebra with at least one basic operation the empty set is closed under all operations, so it *is* a subuniverse but not a subalgebra (algebras have nonempty domain in Bergman's convention). The paper explicitly says at L1628–1630: "we do not allow empty subuniverses and the condition `∅ ≪ A` never holds" and introduces dotted variants for the empty case. **This is the single most pervasive convention hazard in the section.**

### D4. Polynomially complete (PC) algebra (L1103–1108)
"An algebra `(A;F_A)` is called **polynomially complete (PC)** if the clone generated by `F_A` and all constants on `A` is the clone of all operations on `A`."

*Formalization note.* Must fix: does "all operations" include nullary/unary? Standard: all finitary operations `Aⁿ→A`, `n ≥ 1`. Degenerate case `|A| = 1`: trivially PC. `|A|=2` idempotent WNU: cannot be PC (Z₂ is affine). The definition is used only via Lemma 12.

### D5. The class `𝒱ₙ` (L1110–1121)
"By `𝒱ₙ` we denote the class of finite algebras `A = (A; w^A)` whose basic operation `w^A` is an idempotent special WNU operation. Since we only consider finite algebras, `𝒱ₙ` is not a variety."

*Formalization notes.*
- `n` = the arity of `w`, a **global parameter** that is never bound in the text. `𝒱ₙ` is really a family indexed by `n`, and every statement mentioning `𝒱ₙ` implicitly fixes the same `n` as `Zₚ`. In Lean: a section variable `(n : ℕ)`.
- Algebras in `𝒱ₙ` have exactly **one** basic operation. So `Clo(A) = Clo(w)`. Elsewhere (Section 2.3) algebras are arbitrary finite idempotent Taylor algebras with arbitrary signature. Two different algebra notions coexist in the section.
- `𝒱ₙ` is closed under subalgebras, products, quotients *as a class of similar algebras*, but the paper never says so; it is used implicitly (e.g. `A ∈ 𝒱ₙ ⟹ A/σ ∈ 𝒱ₙ`).

### D6. `Z_p` (L1115–1121)
"For a prime `p` by `Z_p` we denote the algebra whose domain is `{0,1,…,p−1}` and whose basic operation `w^{Z_p}` is `x₁+…+x_n (mod p)`. In the paper every algebra `Z_p` belongs to `𝒱ₙ` for a fixed `n`, hence the algebra `Z_p` is uniquely defined."

*Formalization notes.*
- `x₁+…+x_n mod p` is an idempotent WNU **iff `n ≡ 1 (mod p)`**. The paper does not say this. If `n ≢ 1 (mod p)` the operation is not idempotent and `Z_p ∉ 𝒱ₙ`. So the sentence "every algebra `Z_p` belongs to `𝒱ₙ`" is a *silent side condition* `p | n−1` on which primes `p` can ever occur. A formalizer must decide whether `Z_p` is defined for all `p` (and is simply not in `𝒱ₙ` for bad `p`) or only for `p | n−1`. Statements like Lemma 11 ("`A/σ ≅ Z_p` for some prime `p`") implicitly produce a `p` with `p | n−1`.
- Also: is `x₁+…+x_n mod p` *special*? `w(x,…,x,y) = (n−1)x + y = y` when `n ≡ 1 mod p`, so `w(x,…,x,w(x,…,x,y)) = w(x,…,x,y)`; yes, special, given `p | n−1`.

### D7. Subdirect, reflexive, projection, `⩽_sd`, `Sg` (L1148–1162)
- "A relation `R ⊆ A₁×…×A_n` is called **subdirect** if for every `i` the projection of `R` onto the `i`-th coordinate is `A_i`."
- "A relation `R ⊆ Aⁿ` is called **reflexive** if it contains `(a,a,…,a)` for every `a ∈ A`."
- "`pr_{i₁,…,i_s}(R)`" = projection onto those coordinates.
- "`R ⩽_sd A₁×…×A_m`" (`R` is a **subdirect subalgebra**) iff `R` subdirect and `R ⩽ A₁×…×A_m`.
- "For `R ⊆ Aⁿ` by `Sg_A(R)` we denote the minimal subalgebra of `Aⁿ` containing `R`, that is the subalgebra of `Aⁿ` generated from `R`."

*Formalization notes.*
- Subdirect is stated for `n` factors but `⩽_sd` is written with `m`; harmless.
- `Sg_A(R)`: minimal *subalgebra*, so for `R = ∅` in an idempotent algebra `Sg(∅) = ∅`, which is not an algebra. Only used with `R ≠ ∅` (central subuniverse definition, where `C ≠ ∅`) — but see hazard H3.
- Note `Sg_A(R)` for `R ⊆ Aⁿ` is generation **inside `Aⁿ`**, i.e. componentwise application of term operations to tuples.

### D8. Quotients of sets and relations (L1165–1177)
- `a/σ` = the `σ`-class of `a`, for `σ` an **equivalence relation on `A`** (not necessarily a congruence!).
- "For an equivalence relation `σ` on `A` and `B ⊆ A` denote `B/σ = {b/σ | b ∈ B}`."
- "for a relation `R ⊆ Aⁿ` denote `R/σ = {(b₁/σ,…,b_n/σ) | (b₁,…,b_n) ∈ R}`."
- "For a binary relation `σ` and `n ⩾ 2` by `σ^{[n]}` we denote the `n`-ary relation `{(a₁,…,a_n) | ∀i,j ∈ [n]: (a_i,a_j) ∈ σ}`."

*Formalization notes (important).*
- `B/σ` is defined for an arbitrary **subset** `B ⊆ A`, and is a subset of `A/σ` — it is *not* the quotient of `B` by `σ∩B²`, though for `B` a subalgebra and `σ` a congruence the two are canonically isomorphic **as algebras** (`B/(σ∩B²) ≅ B/σ` as a subalgebra of `A/σ`). The paper switches between these two readings freely (e.g. in `LEMReverseHomomorphism`'s proof: "`B'/σ ≅ B/δ`"). A formalizer must fix `B/σ := image of B under the quotient map` and then prove the iso separately.
- `R/σ` is the *image*, so `R/σ` may fail to be "the relation induced by `R`"; in particular `(R/σ)` need not be stable under anything, and `(R∩S)/σ ⊊ R/σ ∩ S/σ` in general. The proof of Lemma 14(fm) explicitly needs `(C₁∩…∩C_t)/δ = C₁/δ∩…∩C_t/δ` and proves it from `(C_i ∘ δ) ∩ B = C_i`. **This is a genuine content step, not notation.**
- `σ^{[n]}` requires `σ` binary but not an equivalence; used with `σ = σ*` in the linear-congruence definition, where `σ*` is only known to be a reflexive symmetric subalgebra (a tolerance) unless it is asserted to be a congruence.

### D9. Composition, `B∘δ`, inverse, linked, bijective (L1181–1194)
- "For two binary relations `δ₁ ⊆ A₁×A₂` and `δ₂ ⊆ A₂×A₃` by `δ₁∘δ₂` we denote `{(a,b) | ∃c: (a,c)∈δ₁ ∧ (c,b)∈δ₂}`."
- "for `B ⊆ A₁` and `δ ⊆ A₁×A₂` put `B∘δ = {c | ∃b∈B: (b,c)∈δ}`."
- "`δ^{-1}(x,y) = δ(y,x)`."
- "A binary subdirect relation `δ ⊆ A×B` is called **linked** if the bipartite graph corresponding to `δ` is connected."
- "A binary subdirect relation `δ ⊆ A₁×A₂` is called **bijective** if `|δ| = |A₁| = |A₂|`."

*Formalization notes.*
- `∘` is written in **diagrammatic/left-to-right** order (`δ₁` first). Mathlib's `Rel.comp` is the same order; `Function.comp` is the opposite. Easy source of silent bugs.
- **linked** requires subdirectness as part of the definition; the commented-out alternative (L1196–1201) is an explicit zig-zag path condition. "The bipartite graph corresponding to `δ`" is left undefined: vertices `A ⊔ B`, edges `δ`. Connectivity of the *whole* bipartite graph. If `A` or `B` is empty the notion degenerates; if `|A|=|B|=1` it is linked. Formalize as: the transitive closure of `δ∘δ^{-1}` is `A²` (equivalently `δ∘δ^{-1}∘…∘δ∘δ^{-1} = A²` for large enough length — exactly the form used in the proof of Lemma 5).
- **bijective** is a cardinality condition; combined with subdirectness it says `δ` is the graph of a bijection `A₁→A₂`. Needs finiteness.
- `B ∘ δ` for `δ` a congruence on `A` and `B ⊆ A` is the `δ`-saturation of `B`; used constantly in 2.3 (Cor 16, 17, Lemma 25).

### D10. Parallelogram property, rectangular, rectangular closure (L1203–1223)
"We say that an `n`-relation `R` **has the parallelogram property** if any permutation of its variables gives a relation `R'` satisfying
for all `ℓ ∈ {1,…,n−1}` and all `a₁,…,a_n,b₁,…,b_n`:
`(a₁,…,a_ℓ,b_{ℓ+1},…,b_n) ∈ R'` and `(b₁,…,b_ℓ,a_{ℓ+1},…,a_n) ∈ R'` and `(b₁,…,b_ℓ,b_{ℓ+1},…,b_n) ∈ R'` ⟹ `(a₁,…,a_n) ∈ R'`."

"We say that **the `i`-th variable of a relation `R` is rectangular**, if for all `a₁,…,a_n,b₁,…,b_n`:
`(a₁,…,a_{i−1},b_i,a_{i+1},…,a_n) ∈ R` and `(b₁,…,b_{i−1},a_i,b_{i+1},…,b_n) ∈ R` and `(b₁,…,b_{i−1},b_i,b_{i+1},…,b_n) ∈ R` ⟹ `(a₁,…,a_n) ∈ R`."

"As it follows from the definitions, if a relation has the parallelogram property then it is rectangular. **The rectangular closure** of a relation `R` is the minimal rectangular relation `R'` containing `R`."

*Formalization notes.*
- "`R` is rectangular" (unqualified, as used in "rectangular closure") is never defined: presumably *every* variable is rectangular. Must be pinned down.
- **Existence of the rectangular closure is asserted, not proved.** Minimality requires that the intersection of two rectangular relations is rectangular — true, since the condition is a Horn implication, so the family is closed under arbitrary intersection and contains `Aⁿ`. Easy, but must be stated as a lemma.
- The parallelogram property quantifies over *all permutations of variables* (`R'` ranges over all coordinate permutations of `R`), and over `ℓ`. That is a nontrivially large statement; note it is used only in Section 3, but the *rectangular closure* is used in Section 2 (Lemma 24).
- "if a relation has the parallelogram property then it is rectangular" is asserted with "as it follows from the definitions" — a **"clearly"**. It is true (take `ℓ = i−1` after permuting `i` to position `i`... actually one needs the permutation moving `i` to the front and `ℓ=1`), but should be a proved lemma.

### D11. Stable under `σ` (L1228–1236)
"For a relation `R ⊆ A₁×…×A_n` and a congruence `σ` on `A_i`, we say that the `i`-th variable of the relation `R` is **stable under `σ`** if `(a₁,…,a_n) ∈ R` and `(a_i,b_i) ∈ σ` imply `(a₁,…,a_{i−1},b_i,a_{i+1},…,a_n) ∈ R`. We say that a relation is **stable under `σ`** if every variable of this relation is stable under `σ`."

*Formalization notes.* For "every variable" to make sense with a single `σ`, all `A_i` must be the same algebra (or `σ` must be a congruence on each). In use: `R ⩽ A²` with `σ` a congruence on `A` (irreducibility), `δ ⩽ D₁²×D₂²` where variables 1,2 are stable under `σ₁` (a congruence on `D₁`) and 3,4 under `σ₂` (bridges). So "stable under `σ`" is per-variable and the global version is only used for `R ⊆ Aⁿ`.

### D12. Irreducible congruence (L1237–1244)
"We say that a congruence `σ` on **A** is **irreducible** if it cannot be represented as an intersection of other binary subalgebras of `A×A` that are stable under `σ`.
Equivalently, a congruence is irreducible if there are no subalgebras `S₁,S₂,…,S_k ⩽ A/σ × A/σ` such that `0_{A/σ} = S₁∩S₂∩…∩S_k` and `0_{A/σ} ≠ S_i` for every `i ∈ [k]`."

*Formalization notes.*
- "**other**" = different from `σ`. Note that any family of `σ`-stable subalgebras whose intersection is `σ` automatically consists of relations **containing `σ`** (because the intersection contains the diagonal, hence so does each `S_i`, and stability then forces `S_i ⊇ σ`). Worth proving as a first lemma; the paper takes it for granted.
- **Is `k = 0` allowed?** With `k=0` the empty intersection is `A²` (resp. `(A/σ)²`), so `σ = A²` would be "reducible". If `k ⩾ 1` is demanded, `σ = A²` is irreducible but `σ*` does not exist. The text is silent. **This decides whether `A²` is an irreducible congruence.** Downstream, `σ*` is used unconditionally for irreducible `σ`, so the sane convention is: irreducible ⟹ `σ ≠ A²` (i.e. allow `k = 0`, or explicitly exclude `σ = A²`).
- The "equivalently" is asserted without proof. It is the standard correspondence between `σ`-stable subalgebras of `A²` and subalgebras of `(A/σ)²`; formalizable but needs the Galois correspondence lemma.
- Note that the `S_i` are **subalgebras of `A×A`, not congruences**. This is what makes "irreducible" strictly stronger than "meet-irreducible in Con(A)".

### D13. `σ*` (L1245–1248)
"Then for an irreducible congruence `σ` on **A** by `σ*` we denote the minimal `δ ⩽ A×A` such that `δ ⊋ σ` and `δ` is stable under `σ`."

*Formalization notes (this is a key hazard).*
- **"minimal" must mean "minimum".** Well-definedness: let `Δ = ⋂{δ : δ ⩽ A², δ ⊋ σ, δ stable under σ}`. `Δ` is a `σ`-stable subalgebra containing `σ`. If `Δ = σ` then `σ` is an intersection of `σ`-stable subalgebras `≠ σ`, contradicting irreducibility; so `Δ ⊋ σ` and `Δ` is the minimum. **So `σ*` is well-defined exactly because `σ` is irreducible** — this is a lemma the paper never states.
- The family can be empty exactly when `σ = A²` (see D12). So the well-definedness lemma needs `σ ≠ A²`.
- `σ*` is **not** in general a congruence: it is reflexive (contains `σ ⊇ 0_A`) and symmetric (because `(σ*)^{-1}` is also a `σ`-stable subalgebra `⊋ σ`, so `σ* ⊆ (σ*)^{-1}`, hence equality), i.e. a **tolerance**, but transitivity is an extra condition — which is exactly clause 2 of the definition of a *linear* congruence. Any Lean development must not type `σ*` as a `Con A`.
- Consequently "**block of `σ*`**" (used in the linear-congruence definition) only makes sense **after** clause 2 has been assumed. The clause order in the definition is load-bearing.

### D14. Bridge (L1251–1261)
"Suppose `σ₁` and `σ₂` are congruences on `D₁` and `D₂`, respectively. A relation `δ ⩽ D₁²×D₂²` is called **a bridge from `σ₁` to `σ₂`** if the following conditions hold:
1. the first two variables of `δ` are stable under `σ₁`,
2. the last two variables of `δ` are stable under `σ₂`,
3. `pr_{1,2}(δ) ⊋ σ₁`, `pr_{3,4}(δ) ⊋ σ₂`,
4. `(a₁,a₂,a₃,a₄) ∈ δ` implies `(a₁,a₂) ∈ σ₁ ⇔ (a₃,a₄) ∈ σ₂`."

Example (L1263–1272): `δ = {(a₁,a₂,a₃,a₄) ∈ Z₄⁴ : a₁−a₂ = 2a₃−2a₄}` is "a bridge from the equality relation (0-congruence) and (mod 2) equivalence relation".

*Formalization notes.*
- `δ ⩽ D₁²×D₂²` means `δ` is a **subalgebra of `D₁×D₁×D₂×D₂`** (4-ary relation), not a binary relation between pairs.
- Condition 3 uses `⊋` (strict): a bridge is by definition *nontrivial* on both sides. Note `pr_{1,2}(δ) ⊇ σ₁` is not automatic; strictness is.
- Condition 4 is an iff *inside* the implication.
- No reflexivity is required; "reflexive bridge" appears later (Lemma 9) and means `(a,a,a,a) ∈ δ` for all `a` — **never defined in Section 2**; it is only the general definition of reflexive relation (D7) applied to `δ ⊆ D⁴` with `D₁=D₂=D`. Pin down: reflexive bridge presupposes `D₁ = D₂`.
- The `Z₄` example is *wrong in wording*: "a bridge from the equality relation **and** (mod 2) equivalence relation" (should be "**to**"), and it is a bridge from `0` to `mod 2` only if one checks conditions 1–4 (it is: `a₁−a₂=2(a₃−a₄)` forces `a₁=a₂ ⇔ a₃≡a₄ mod 2`). Note the ambient algebra must be `Z₄` **as a member of `𝒱ₙ`**; but `Z₄` is not a prime-order `Z_p`, so this example lives outside the `Z_p` notation of D6 — it is an illustration only, using the algebra `(Z₄; x₁+…+x_n mod 4)`.

### D15. `δ̃` (L1277–1279)
"For a bridge `δ` by `δ̃` we denote the binary relation defined by `δ̃(x,y) = δ(x,x,y,y)`."

*Formalization notes.* `δ̃ = {(x,y) : (x,x,y,y) ∈ δ}` — a *substitution*, not a projection. `δ̃ ⊆ D₁×D₂`, and it is a subalgebra of `D₁×D₂`. It can be empty (nothing forces `δ̃ ≠ ∅`), which matters: Lemma 5 requires `δ̃` linked, and "linked" presupposes subdirect, hence nonempty (for nonempty `D`).

### D16. Composition of bridges (L1281–1287)
"`δ(x₁,x₂,z₁,z₂) = ∃y₁∃y₂ δ₁(x₁,x₂,y₁,y₂) ∧ δ₂(y₁,y₂,z₁,z₂)`. We can prove (Lemma 28) that `δ` is a bridge from `σ₀` to `σ₂` whenever the congruences `σ₁`, `σ₂`, and `σ₃` are irreducible. Moreover, `δ̃ = δ̃₁ ∘ δ̃₂`."

*Formalization notes.* **Index mismatch:** the composition goes `σ₀ → σ₁ → σ₂`, but the hypothesis names `σ₁, σ₂, σ₃`. Lemma 28 (`LEMBridgeComposition`, necessaryClaims.tex:23) uses `σ₁,σ₂,σ₃` consistently. So the sentence in 2.1 should say "whenever `σ₀,σ₁,σ₂` are irreducible". A blueprint should use Lemma 28's indexing. Also, `δ̃ = δ̃₁ ∘ δ̃₂` is a nontrivial extra conclusion (the `⊇` direction is trivial; `⊆` is not).

### D17. Perfect linear congruence (L1295–1304)
"A congruence `σ` on `A = (A;w)` is called **a perfect linear congruence** if it is irreducible and there exists `ζ ⩽ A×A×Z_p` such that `pr_{1,2} ζ = σ*` and `(a₁,a₂,b) ∈ ζ` implies that `(a₁,a₂) ∈ σ ⇔ (b = 0)`."

*Formalization notes.*
- `A = (A;w)` — so this notion is stated only for one-basic-operation algebras, i.e. `A ∈ 𝒱ₙ` (Lemmas 5, 27 both require `A ∈ 𝒱ₙ`).
- `p` is existentially quantified (a prime) and `Z_p` must be in `𝒱ₙ` (so `p | n−1`).
- `ζ ⩽ A×A×Z_p` is a subalgebra of the product — so `w` acts on all three coordinates; this is where the `Z_p` structure bites.
- Used only via Lemmas 5, 11, 27. Note "perfect linear congruence" is **not** in the list of the six types; it is an auxiliary notion, and the relation "perfect linear ⟹ linear" is never stated explicitly in Section 2 (it follows from Lemma 7 by building a bridge from `ζ`, but the paper does not do it here).

### D18. Absorbing / binary absorbing / ternary absorbing subuniverse (L1328–1342)
"We say `B` is **an absorbing subuniverse** of an algebra `A` if there exists `t ∈ Clo(A)` such that `t(B,B,…,B,A,B,…,B) ⊆ B` for any position of `A`. Also in this case we say that **`B` absorbs `A` with a term `t`**.
If the operation `t` can be chosen binary then we say that `B` is **a binary absorbing subuniverse** of `A`. To shorten sometimes we will write **BA** instead of binary absorbing. If `t` can be chosen ternary the we call `B` **a ternary absorbing subuniverse**."

*Formalization notes.*
- **`B` must independently be a subuniverse** (`B ⩽ A`). The absorption identity alone gives closure under `t` only. (A deleted line at L1329 confirms the intent: "Let `B=(B;F_B)` be a subalgebra of `A=(A;F_A)`".) In Lean: `structure Absorbs (B : Subuniverse A)`.
- `t ∈ Clo(A)` = **term** operation (not polynomial). Under the standing idempotence assumption this matters.
- "for any position of `A`" = for every `i`, `t(B,…,B,A,B,…,B) ⊆ B` with `A` in position `i`. **One single `t` for all positions.**
- The **same term for different algebras** is not required by this definition, but *is* required by the external results it is used with (see Hazard H7).
- `B = ∅` vacuously absorbs (`t(∅,…,A,…,∅) = ∅`), and `B = A` always absorbs. Hence "proper nonempty" qualifiers below.
- `t` must have arity `⩾ 2` for the definition to be non-vacuous; for arity 1 the condition reads `t(A) ⊆ B`. The paper never restricts arity here; `LemAbsorptionImpliesEssential` (§5) uses `n ⩾ 2`.

### D19. Central subuniverse (L1345–1351)
"A subuniverse `C` of `A` is called **central** if it is an absorbing subuniverse and for every `a ∈ A∖C` we have `(a,a) ∉ Sg_A(({a}×C) ∪ (C×{a}))`."

*Formalization notes.*
- `Sg_A(X)` for `X ⊆ A²` — generated subalgebra of `A×A`.
- `C = ∅`: `Sg(∅) = ∅` so the condition holds vacuously and `∅` is "central". `C = A`: vacuous, `A` is central in `A`. So both extremes are central; properness/nonemptiness is imposed externally.
- Central ⟹ absorbing is part of the definition; central ⟹ ternary absorbing is Lemma 6 (external). The converse fails in general (stated, with a pointer to minimal Taylor algebras).

### D20. BA and center free (L1362–1364)
"We say that an algebra `A` is **BA and center free** if `A` has no proper nonempty binary absorbing subuniverse or proper nonempty central subuniverse."

*Formalization notes.*
- English ambiguity: "has no X or Y" = "has neither X nor Y". Formalize as a conjunction of two negations.
- **A one-element algebra is BA and center free** (vacuously: no proper nonempty subset). This matters: clause 3 of the `<_D` definition does not by itself force `|B/σ| > 1`; that comes from `C ⊊ B`. Compare `LEMMainExistenceOfIrreducibleCongruence` (§5:2185) which explicitly adds `|B/σ| > 1`.
- The empty algebra: also vacuously BA and center free. Whether `∅` is allowed as `B` in `B/σ` matters in Cor 18(m1) etc.

### D21. Linear congruence (L1366–1380)
"A congruence `σ` on `A` is called **linear** if
1. `σ` is irreducible
2. `σ*` is a congruence
3. there exist prime `p` and `S ⩽ (σ*)^{[4]}` such that for any block `B` of `σ*` there exists `n ⩾ 0` with `(B/σ; S ∩ (B/σ)⁴) ≅ (Z_p^n; x₁−x₂=x₃−x₄)`.

Notice that the relation `S` above is a bridge from `σ` to `σ` such that `S̃ = pr_{1,2}(S) = pr_{3,4}(S) = σ*`."

*Formalization notes (several).*
- `S ⩽ (σ*)^{[4]}` means `S` is a subalgebra of `A⁴` contained in `(σ*)^{[4]}` (all 4-tuples pairwise `σ*`-related). Since clause 2 makes `σ*` a congruence, `(σ*)^{[4]} = ⋃_{B block of σ*} B⁴`.
- `S ∩ (B/σ)⁴` is an **abuse**: `S` is a relation on `A`, `(B/σ)⁴` a relation on `A/σ`. The intended meaning is `(S/σ) ∩ (B/σ)⁴`, and for that to be well behaved one needs **`S` stable under `σ` in all four variables** — which is *not* among the stated conditions but *is* implied by the "Notice" sentence (bridges are stable). Formalizers must add `S` stable under `σ` as an explicit hypothesis (or work with `S/σ`).
- `n ⩾ 0` is **per block** and reuses the letter `n` already used for the WNU arity in `𝒱ₙ`. Rename.
- `p` is **global across all blocks**; `n` varies. `(Z_p^n; x₁−x₂=x₃−x₄)` is the 4-ary relation on the group `Z_p^n`.
- `≅` is isomorphism of **relational structures** (a bijection preserving the 4-ary relation in both directions), not of algebras.
- The "Notice" is an **unproved claim** used in the proof of Lemma 7 (§5:1346, direction 1⇒2: "By property 3 of linear congruence we have a bridge `δ` such that `δ̃ ⊇ σ`" — and it must be `⊋`, since `δ̃ = σ* ⊋ σ`). So the "Notice" is load-bearing and needs a proof: that `S` satisfies bridge conditions 1–4 and `S̃ = pr₁₂(S) = pr₃₄(S) = σ*`.
- Degenerate blocks: if the block `B` has `|B/σ| = 1` then `n = 0`. So clause 3 is compatible with `σ* = σ`? No — `σ* ⊋ σ` always, so at least one block has `|B/σ| ⩾ 2`, forcing `p^n ⩾ 2` there.

### D22. PC congruence (L1382–1386)
"An irreducible congruence is called a **PC congruence** if it is not linear.
Notice that a congruence `σ` is an irreducible/PC/linear congruence if and only if `0_{A/σ}` is an irreducible/PC/Linear congruence."

*Formalization notes.*
- PC := irreducible ∧ ¬linear. Hence **every irreducible congruence is linear or PC, exclusively** — this decidable dichotomy is used everywhere (e.g. type `D` = type `L` ∪ type `PC`).
- The "Notice" (transfer to `A/σ`) is an **unproved compatibility claim**, used implicitly all over Section 5 (e.g. the reduction of statements about `σ` to statements about `0`). It requires: the lattice/relational correspondence between `σ`-stable subalgebras of `A²` and subalgebras of `(A/σ)²`, plus that `(0_{A/σ})* = σ*/σ`. Formalizing this correspondence early is probably the single highest-leverage infrastructure lemma for the whole section.

### D23. The six subuniverse types (L1504–1525)
"Suppose `∅ ≠ C ⊊ B ⩽ A`. We write
- `C <^A_{BA} B` if `C` is a BA subuniverse of `B`.
- `C <^A_{C} B` if `C` is a central subuniverse of `B`.
- `C <^A_{D} B` if there exists an irreducible congruence `σ` such that
  1. `B² ⊆ σ*`;
  2. `C = B ∩ E` for some block `E` of `σ`;
  3. `B/σ` is BA and center free.
- `C <^A_{L} B` if `C <^A_D B` and the congruence `σ` from the definition of `<^A_D` is linear.
- `C <^A_{PC} B` if `C <^A_D B` and the congruence `σ` from the definition of `<^A_D` is a PC congruence.
- `C <^A_{S} B` if there exists a BA and central (simultaneously) subuniverse `D` in `B` such that `D ⩽ C`."

*Formalization notes (the heart of the section).*
- **Standing hypothesis `∅ ≠ C ⊊ B ⩽ A` applies to all six clauses.** So `<_T` is always *proper* and *nonempty*. This is why `≪` and the dotted variants exist.
- `σ` in the `D`-clause is a congruence on **`A`** (the superscript algebra), *not* on `B`. This global-ness is the paper's headline novelty ("strong subalgebras and linear congruences that are defined globally"). `σ*` is computed in `A`. `B/σ ⊆ A/σ`. This is the crucial difference from Zhuk 2017/2020.
- "**block `E` of `σ`**" = a `σ`-equivalence class **in `A`**, i.e. `E = a/σ` for some `a ∈ A`. Since `A` is idempotent, `E ⩽ A`, so `C = B ∩ E ⩽ B`. `C ≠ ∅` and `C ≠ B` come from the standing hypothesis, and `C ≠ B` forces `|B/σ| > 1`.
- Clause 1 `B² ⊆ σ*` says `B` lies inside one `σ*`-"block" (when `σ*` is a congruence) — but `σ*` need not be a congruence for a general irreducible `σ`, so clause 1 is stated at the level of the tolerance `σ*`.
- Clause 3: `B/σ` is a subalgebra of `A/σ` and is asked to be BA-and-center-free **as an algebra in its own right**.
- **`<_S` needs `D ≠ ∅` and this is not written.** If `D = ∅` were allowed, `∅` is both BA and central (D18/D19 notes), and `∅ ⊆ C` always, so `<_S` would be universally true. The intended reading is `D <_{BA,C} B` (nonempty, proper) — which the equivalent formulation of "S-free" (below) confirms. **Must be fixed in a blueprint.**
- `D ⩽ C` here means `D ⊆ C` (`D` is a subuniverse of `B` contained in `C`), not "subalgebra of the algebra `C`" — although both hold.
- `<_L` and `<_PC` "the congruence `σ` **from the definition**": `σ` is existentially quantified in `<_D`, so this phrase must be read as "there exists a linear (resp. PC) `σ` witnessing `C <^A_D B`". Different witnesses may have different types; the notation `C <^A_{T(σ)} B` (below) makes the witness explicit. **Formalize `<_D` as a relation carrying the witness, or define `<_{T(σ)}` primitively and `<_T := ∃σ, <_{T(σ)}`.** The paper effectively does the latter.

### D24. `<^A_{T(σ)}`, dividing/linear/PC subuniverse & congruence, S-free (L1528–1566)
- "When we want to specify what congruence was used in the definition we write `C <^A_{T(σ)} B`. Sometimes, we also put a congruence there even if `T ∈ {BA,C,S}`, which means that `σ` is a full congruence."
- "If `C <^A_T B` then we say that `C` is **a subuniverse of `B` of type `T`**."
- "Sometimes we also call `B` **a dividing subuniverse** for the type `D`, **a linear subuniverse** for the type `L`, and **a PC subuniverse** for the type `PC`."
- "Also, we say that `σ` is a **dividing/linear/PC congruence for `B ⩽ A`** if `C <^A_{T(σ)} B` for some `C` and `T = D/L/PC`."
- "We say that an algebra `A` is **S-free** if there is no `D ⩽ A` such that `D <_{BA} A` and `D <_C A`. Equivalently, an algebra `A` is S-free if there does not exist `C <_S A`."
- "Sometimes instead of `C <^A_{BA} B`, `C <^A_C B`, and `C <^A_S B` we write `C <_{BA} B`, `C <_C B`, and `C <_S B`, which is justified because `A` is irrelevant to the definition."
- "Also, we write `C <_{BA,C} B` meaning that `C <_{BA} B` and `C <_C B`."

*Formalization notes.*
- "`σ` is a **full** congruence" means `σ = A²`; so `<_{BA(A²)}`, etc. Note `A²` is *not* irreducible under the natural convention (D12), so the `σ` slot for BA/C/S types is purely bookkeeping. In Theorem 21 the `σ_i` for BA/C types are never used in the conclusion — but the statement quantifies over them, so a formalization must allow a dummy.
- Note the terminological slip: "we also call **`B`** a dividing subuniverse for the type `D`" — from the rest of the paper it is clear that the *type of the pair* is meant; `B` is the bigger one. Zhuk elsewhere calls `C` the subuniverse. Reading `C <_D B`: `C` is the dividing subuniverse *of* `B`. The sentence as printed is at best confusing.
- "dividing congruence for `B ⩽ A`" quantifies out `C`: `σ` is a dividing congruence for `B` iff `∃C, C <^A_{D(σ)} B`, equivalently iff clauses 1–3 hold **and** some block meets `B` in a proper nonempty subset (automatic once `|B/σ|>1`).
- **S-free equivalence** ("Equivalently, …") is an unproved two-line claim; it needs the `D ≠ ∅` fix above.

### D25. `≪` (`\lll`) (L1588–1605)
"We write `C ≪^A B` if there exist `B₀,B₁,…,B_n ⊆ B` and `T₁,…,T_n ∈ {BA, C, S, D}` such that
`C = B_n <^A_{T_n} B_{n−1} <^A_{T_{n−1}} … <^A_{T_2} B_1 <^A_{T_1} B_0 = B`.
Notice that `n` can be 0 and the relation `≪^A` is reflexive.
We say that a congruence **comes from `C ≪^A B`** if it is one of the dividing congruences used in the sequence `C ≪^A B`.
We usually write `B ≪ A` instead of `B ≪^A A`.
We write `C ⩽^A_{T(σ)} B` if `C = B` or `C <^A_{T(σ)} B`."

*Formalization notes.*
- The printed chain has stray `<` signs (`<^A_{T_{n−1}} < … <^A_{T_2} <B_1`) — a typo; the intended chain is the obvious one.
- Types allowed in a `≪` chain are `{BA, C, S, D}` — `L` and `PC` are special cases of `D`, so nothing is lost.
- **`≪` is a reflexive-transitive closure but it is *not* canonical**: "a congruence comes from `C ≪^A B`" depends on the *chosen witnessing chain*. Several §5 proofs (e.g. `LEMIntersectionPCLinearIsGood`, `LEMPreserveLinkdnessOneStepAUX`) speak of "the intersection of all irreducible congruences coming from `C ≪ A`" and of "`k` and `ℓ` chosen minimal". **A Lean formalization must make `≪` a data-carrying inductive (the chain), not a Prop, or must carry the chain as an explicit argument.** This is a major architectural decision.
- Transitivity of `≪` is used freely (proof of Lemma 20(i)) but never stated.
- `B_i ⊆ B` is stated; each `B_i ⩽ A` follows from the `<_T` clauses.

### D26. Multi-types `ML`, `MPC`, `MD` (L1609–1616)
"Let us introduce the types `ML, MPC, MD` of subuniverses. Suppose `T ∈ {L, PC, D}`. We write `C <^A_{MT} B` if `C ≠ ∅` and `C = C₁ ∩ … ∩ C_t`, where `C_i <^A_T B` for every `i ∈ [t]`."

*Formalization notes.*
- `t ⩾ 1` must be intended (an empty intersection would be `B`, and `<` is proper). Since each `C_i ⊊ B`, `C ⊊ B` automatically, so `<_{MT}` is strict; `⩽_{MT}` then adds `C = B` (used in Lemma 14(fm),(bm), Cor 15(m), Cor 18(m),(m1), Lemma 23).
- The `C_i` may use **different** congruences `σ_i`, all of the same type family `T`.
- `MD` is the union-closure over both `L` and `PC` witnesses.

### D27. Dotted variants (L1628–1637)
"Notice that we do not allow empty subuniverses and the condition `∅ ≪ A` never holds. Nevertheless, sometimes we need to allow an empty set. In this case we add a dot above and write `B ≪̇ A` meaning that `B ≪ A` or `B = ∅`. With the same meaning we use dots in the following notations `C <̇^A_T B` or `C ⩽̇^A_T B`."

*Formalization notes.*
- `X ≪̇ A :⇔ X ≪ A ∨ X = ∅`; `C <̇_T B :⇔ C <_T B ∨ C = ∅`; `C ⩽̇_T B :⇔ C = B ∨ C <_T B ∨ C = ∅`.
- Note `⩽̇` is used in Lemma 19 with an implicit ambient (`⩽̇_T A₁`), and in Cor 18(m),(m1) with `⩽̇_{MT}`. The dotted `MT` variant is never spelled out; by analogy `C ⩽̇_{MT} B :⇔ C = B ∨ C <_{MT} B ∨ C = ∅`.

**Lemma 5** (`LEMMakePerfectCongruenceFromLinked`, main.tex:1310) — **§2.4 (proved, necessaryClaims.tex:36–57)**
> Suppose `σ` is a irreducible congruence on `A ∈ 𝒱ₙ`, `δ` is a bridge from `σ` to `σ` such that `δ̃` is linked. Then `σ` is a perfect linear congruence.

Proof: define `δ^{-1}(y₁,y₂,x₁,x₂) = δ(x₁,x₂,y₁,y₂)`; since `δ̃` is linked, `δ̃ ∘ δ̃^{-1} ∘ … ∘ δ̃ ∘ δ̃^{-1}` (`2N` factors) `= A²` for sufficiently large `N`; compose `2N` bridges by Lemma 28 to get `δ'` with `δ̃' = A²`; apply Lemma 27. *Gaps:* (i) that `δ^{-1}` is again a bridge from `σ` to `σ` is asserted, not proved (needs `δ̃^{-1} = (δ̃)^{-1}` and conditions 1–4 transfer); (ii) "for sufficiently large `N`" — the passage from "linked" to "some fixed even composition length equals `A²`" needs finiteness and a monotonicity argument; (iii) `δ̃ ∘ δ̃^{-1}` requires `δ̃` subdirect (part of "linked").

**Lemma 12** (`LEMPCOnTheTopIsEasy`, main.tex:1465) — **§5 (StrongSubalgebras.tex:2764)**

**Lemma 29** (`LEMNoAbsCenterPCInLinearAlgebra`) — **proved in place (4 lines)**
> Suppose `B ⩽ Z_p × … × Z_p`. Then there does not exist `C <_T B` such that `T ∈ {BA, C}`.

Proof: "It is sufficient to check for any term `τ` that `τ^{Z_p}(a,…,a,x)` takes all the values if the last variable is not dummy. Hence, there cannot be an absorbing subuniverse in `Z_p × … × Z_p`."
*Gaps (this proof is genuinely sketchy):* (i) the statement is about subalgebras `B` of a power, but the argument is about the power itself; (ii) `T ∈ {BA, C}` but the argument shows no *absorbing* subuniverse at all — for `C` one also needs Lemma 6 or the definition; (iii) `\mathcal T` vs `T` typo in the LaTeX (`C<_{\mathcal T} B` with `T ∈ {…}`); (iv) the key claim "τ(a,…,a,x) takes all values" needs that every term of `Z_p` is affine and idempotent, hence `τ(a,…,a,x) = λx + const` with `λ ≠ 0` when `x` is not dummy.

# 4. Formalization hazards

**H1 — `≪` is data, not a proposition.** "A congruence *comes from* `C ≪^A B`" (D25) refers to the witnessing chain, and §5 proofs pick "`k` and `ℓ` minimal" (`LEMIntersectionPCLinearIsGood`) and form "the intersection of all irreducible congruences coming from `C_i ≪ A_i`" (`LEMPreserveLinkdnessOneStepAUX`). Two different chains give two different sets of congruences. **Decision required at architecture time:** make `≪` an inductive family carrying the chain (`StrongChain A B C`) and define the Prop as `Nonempty`, then restate every lemma that mentions "coming from" over the chain. Anything less will not typecheck against §5's proofs.

**H2 — Empty set discipline.** `∅` is a subuniverse (closed vacuously), is absorbing, is central, and `Sg(∅)=∅`. The paper's convention is "no empty subuniverses" plus dotted variants. Every one of the six type relations must therefore be defined with `C ≠ ∅ ∧ C ⊊ B` baked in, and the `<_S` clause must be repaired to demand `D ≠ ∅` (otherwise `<_S` is universally true — see D23). In Lean, either use `{ s : Set A // s.Nonempty ∧ closed }` or carry nonemptiness hypotheses everywhere; the dotted relations are then `Option`-like disjunctions.

**H3 — `σ*` is not a congruence and needs irreducibility to exist.** `σ*` is a minimum, and its existence is *equivalent in practice* to irreducibility (D13). It is a tolerance (reflexive, symmetric, subalgebra of `A²`), not transitive in general. "Block of `σ*`" is only legitimate under clause 2 of the linear definition. **Do not type `σ*` as `Con A`.** Also, whether `σ = A²` counts as irreducible is left open by the text (`k = 0` in D12) and must be decided; the only coherent choice is to exclude it.

**H4 — Congruences live on `A`, quotients are taken of subsets.** `σ` in `<^A_{D(σ)}` is a congruence on the *top* algebra `A`; `B/σ` for a subalgebra `B ⩽ A` is the *image* in `A/σ`, which is canonically isomorphic to but not equal to `B/(σ ∩ B²)`. §5 uses both readings interchangeably (`LEMReverseHomomorphism`: "`B'/σ ≅ B/δ`"). Fix one definition and prove the transfer lemma once. Related: `(⋂C_i)/δ = ⋂(C_i/δ)` is **false in general** and is proved on the fly in Lemma 14(fm) using `(C_i∘δ)∩B = C_i`.

**H5 — Statements with `T` unquantified.** In 2.3 an unspecified `T` ranges over all six types (`{BA,C,S,PC,L,D}`), including `D`. Several sub-results in §5 are proved only for subsets: `LEMReverseHomomorphism` is stated for `T ∈ {BA,C,S,L,D}` (**`PC` is missing from the list although the proof covers `{PC,L}`** — a typo); `LEMFactorByDelta` covers `T ∈ {PC,L}` only, yet Lemma 14(ft) is applied for all `T`. The `D` case is fine because every irreducible congruence is either linear or PC, but that dichotomy (D22) must be a proved lemma.

**H6 — The unproved "Notice"s.** Three assertions in Section 2 are stated as remarks, used as lemmas, and never proved:
  (a) "the relation `S` above is a bridge from `σ` to `σ` such that `S̃ = pr₁₂(S) = pr₃₄(S) = σ*`" (L1378–1380) — used in the proof of Lemma 7;
  (b) "a congruence `σ` is an irreducible/PC/linear congruence if and only if `0_{A/σ}` is …" (L1384–1386) — used pervasively;
  (c) "an algebra `A` is S-free iff there does not exist `C <_S A`" (L1552–1554).
  Plus the "equivalently" reformulation of irreducibility (D12) and "parallelogram ⟹ rectangular" (D10). All are provable, all are real work.

**H7 — Lemma 19 (`LEMBACenterImplies`) is misstated.** As printed: "`R ⩽ A₁×…×A_n`, `C_i ⩽_T A_i`, `T ∈ {BA,C}` ⟹ `pr₁(R ∩ (C₁×…×C_N)) ⩽̇_T A₁`."
  - **Missing hypothesis `pr₁(R) = A₁`.** The cited sources (`2005.00593.txt:1383, 1539`) both read "Suppose `R ≤ A₁×…×A_n`, **`pr₁(R) = A₁`**, `B_i ≤_{BA(t)} A_i` (resp. `≤_C`) …, and `B = pr₁(R ∩ (B₁×…×B_n))`. Then `B ≤_{BA(t)} A₁` (resp. `≤_C A₁`)."
  - Counterexample to the printed version: `A₁ = A₂ = Z_p`, `R = {(0,0)}`, `C_i = A_i` (legal, since `⩽_T` allows equality). Then `pr₁(R∩(C₁×C₂)) = {0}`, which by Lemma 29 is **not** a BA subuniverse of `Z_p`.
  - **The BA case additionally needs a common term.** The source is stated with `BA(t)` — the *same* binary term `t` for all `i`. Zhuk's own terminology in `2005.00593` distinguishes "BA types with probably different term operations" as merely *similar*. A formalization of Lemma 19 for `T = BA` must either fix `t` or prove a term-amalgamation lemma.
  - `C_N` vs `C_n` is a typo (present in the PDF too).

**H8 — Corollary 15 is never proved.** `CORPropagationModuloCongruence` (P3) is used four times in §5 and once in §3 but has no proof anywhere and is not restated. It is Lemma 14 applied to `A → A/δ` plus the identification `f(C) = C/δ`, `f^{-1}` etc. Formalizers should treat it as a definitional corollary but must supply the (easy) bridge lemma `f(C) = C/δ` for the canonical projection.

**H9 — Typos that change meaning.** Beyond H7:
  - Lemma 14(b): "`C' ≪^{A'} B' ⟹ f^{-1}(C') ≪ f^{-1}(B)`" — `B` should be `B'`.
  - Lemma 14(bt): superscript `A'` on the conclusion `f^{-1}(C') <^{A'}_{…} f^{-1}(B')` should be `A`.
  - Lemma 14(bm): `C`, `B` unprimed on the right-hand side; should be `C'`, `B'`.
  - Lemma 14(ft): `f(C) <_S f(B)` uses roman `S` rather than the type macro `\TS`.
  - Theorem 21(l) / Corollary 22(l): "a bridge `δ` **from** `σ_k` **and** `σ_ℓ`" — should be "from `σ_k` **to** `σ_ℓ`".
  - `≪` chain in D25 has stray `<` symbols.
  - D16: the composition hypothesis names `σ₁,σ₂,σ₃` for a chain `σ₀→σ₁→σ₂`.
  - The `Z₄` bridge example: "from the equality relation **and** (mod 2) equivalence relation".

**H10 — `𝒱ₙ` vs general Taylor algebras.** Section 2 silently mixes two settings: 2.1's `𝒱ₙ`/`Z_p` material (single special-WNU basic operation) and 2.3's "arbitrary finite idempotent algebra with a WNU term operation" (arbitrary signature). Lemmas 5, 11, 27 need `𝒱ₙ`; Lemma 29 needs `Z_p`; everything in 2.3 is signature-agnostic. Two typeclass hierarchies, or one with an extra hypothesis. Also `n` in `𝒱ₙ` is a free global parameter, and it collides with the `n ⩾ 0` of the linear-congruence definition and with the arity `n` in the `[k]`/relations notation.

**H11 — `Z_p ∈ 𝒱ₙ` requires `p | n−1`.** Never stated (D6). Every existential "`≅ Z_p` for some prime `p`" therefore implicitly ranges over a restricted set of primes. If a formalization defines `Z_p` for all `p`, the statement `A/σ ≅ Z_p` (Lemma 11) is about the *algebra* `Z_p` whose operation must be the same `n`-ary WNU — so the constraint is unavoidable and should be discharged up front.

**H12 — Quantifier structure of `<_L` / `<_PC`.** `<_D` existentially quantifies `σ`; `<_L`/`<_PC` say "the congruence `σ` from the definition of `<_D` is linear/PC". Read literally this is ill-formed (there may be several witnesses). The `<_{T(σ)}` notation shows the intended primitive is the **witness-carrying** relation; `<_T := ∃σ, <_{T(σ)}`. Theorem 21 and Lemma 25 quantify over the witnesses `σ_i` in their hypotheses *and* conclusions, so the witness must be an explicit argument throughout. This also means `C <_D B` does **not** imply "`C <_L B` or `C <_PC B` for the same reason" unless one fixes the witness — though it does imply the disjunction (pick a witness; it is linear or PC).

**H13 — "minimal"/"maximal" existence.** Three objects are introduced as minimal/maximal without an existence argument: `σ*` (D13, H3), the rectangular closure (D10), and "`σ` is a **maximal** congruence on `A` such that `(C₁∘σ)∩B₂ = ∅`" (Lemma 25). The last needs finiteness of `Con(A)` and that the family is nonempty (`0_A` works since `C₁ ∩ B₂ = ∅`) — fine, but it is *maximal*, not *maximum*, and Lemma 25's conclusion is an equality `σ = ω₁∩…∩ω_s`, so maximality is used essentially.

**H14 — Vocabulary drift with the cited papers.** "Strong subuniverse" in `zhuk2021strong` (= `2005.00593`) means *BA, central, or PC* in the **local/old** sense; in `2404.01080` the types are redefined **globally** (congruences on the top algebra `A`). §5 nonetheless imports `zhuk2021strong` Lemmas 6.1, 6.8, 6.24, 6.25, Theorem 6.9, 6.15 and applies them to the new notions. For BA/central the notions genuinely coincide (they don't involve congruences), which is why the imports are sound — but a formalizer must check each import against the *old* definition, not the new one. `LEMBACenterSPossibleIntersections` (§5:87) is cited as `zhuk2021strong` Lemma 6.25 but is restated for `T ∈ {BA,C,S}` whereas 6.25 is about the old "strong subuniverses" including PC; the `S` type does not exist in the old paper.
